# Introduction to Analytical / Execution Questions

> Analytical/execution PM interview questions test your ability to set goals, reason about data, and make defensible decisions under uncertainty.

According to research among top companies, the most common PM interview question today is some variation of **"Define the north-star metric / success metrics for X."** A common scenario is for the question to start by defining metrics, then add complexity by asking a conflicting-metric follow-up.

---

## How Analytical Has Changed

**Two shifts have made these questions meaningfully harder:**

**1. Agentic AI products have introduced new sources of metric noise.**
Bots, AI assistants, and automated workflows can now inflate your engagement numbers in ways older frameworks weren't built to catch.

**2. AI products require metric categories that classic engagement funnels don't cover.**
Quality, trust, and helpfulness don't map cleanly to clicks or time on page. Interviewers at OpenAI, Apple, and Perplexity are now actively testing whether you understand that difference.

---

## What to Expect

| Phase | Time | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor question** | 20–25 minutes | Define success for a product or experiment |
| **Follow-up** | 5–10 minutes | Interviewer puts pressure on the metrics you just defined |

---

## Example Anchor Questions

- *"How would you determine success for Instagram Reels?"* — Meta, Instagram
- *"Why do you think Meta built the bell icon for the mobile app? How would you set goals for it and measure its success?"* — Meta
- *"We launched a Gemini tutoring feature for complex skills, and the feedback is highly polarized. Fewer than 10% think it's magical, and most think it's useless. How do you fix it?"* — Google DeepMind

---

## The Three Follow-Up Types

Most interviews end with one of these:

| Follow-up Type | What It Tests | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Root cause analysis** | Can you diagnose a dropped metric systematically? | *"For Instagram Reels, if prioritizing Reels would cannibalize Stories, what would you do?"* |
| **Conflicting metrics** | Can you reason through two metrics moving in opposite directions? | *"Notification engagement is up week over week for six weeks across all users and geographies. But time on site is stable or declining. What do you do?"* |
| **The ship decision** | Can you make a defensible call when a guardrail metric is down? | *"The LLM fails occasionally right now. What do you launch?"* |

---

## What Interviewers Are Looking For

**Systematic diagnosis.**
Diagnose a broken metric or ambiguous data signal using structured reasoning — not jumping to the first plausible explanation.

**Metric choice with defense.**
Choose the right metric for what's being built and defend that choice — including knowing where classic engagement metrics can mislead for AI products.

**Actionable plan.**
Translate your analysis into a concrete, actionable plan — not just a directional call.

**Real-time communication.**
Communicate your reasoning clearly enough that the interviewer can follow along and engage as a thinking partner.

**Genuine curiosity.**
Show real interest in the problem, rather than executing a memorized framework.

---

## Meeting the Senior Bar

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Solid** | Names the tradeoff, lists considerations on both sides, and reaches a conclusion — even if hedged: *"I'd probably lean toward shipping, but I'd want to keep an eye on the guardrail metric."* |
| **Senior+** | Hypotheses are drawn from a fuller picture, including AI-specific failure modes. The metric choice is defended against real alternatives and paired with a guardrail. The plan is fully specified: who gets it first, what the rollout sequence is, and the exact condition that triggers reversal. Treats the interviewer as a thinking partner throughout. Questions asked reveal genuine curiosity — not just what's needed to move to the next step. |
