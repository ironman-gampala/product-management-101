# Rubric for Estimation Interviews

> Estimation questions generally assess two things: **critical thinking** and **culture fit**. Companies often use a five-point scoring system ranging from "Missing / Very Weak" to "Very Strong."

---

## How the Rubric Works

Interviewers score each dimension on a five-point scale during each round, then share feedback during the hiring discussion. The six core dimensions are split into two categories:

| Category | Dimensions |
|---|---|
| **Critical Thinking** | Problem-Solving, Comfort with Numbers, Ability to Identify Possible Errors |
| **Culture Fit** | Communication, Collaboration, Creativity |

---

## Dimension 1 — Problem-Solving

One of the core pillars of Google's interview rubric ("general cognitive ability"). Scores whether you can structure an ambiguous problem before jumping to an answer.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to ask questions; rushed into an answer |
| **Weak** | Serious errors or significant guidance needed in scoping the problem |
| **Neutral** | Scoped the problem, but missed key element(s) |
| **Strong** | Asked good questions and used answers effectively to scope a solvable problem |
| **Very Strong** | Asked insightful questions; set up an easy calculation and an accurate answer |

---

## Dimension 2 — Comfort with Numbers

PMs in big tech often do quick calculations on the fly. This dimension scores numerical fluency — the ability to check assumptions and assess opportunities with numbers in real time.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to calculate on the fly or show competence with numerical relationships |
| **Weak** | Attempts to quantify answer were sloppy or needed lots of guidance |
| **Neutral** | Arrived at a fair answer with a nudge or two |
| **Strong** | Calculations were accurate and quick; approximations were helpful and appropriate |
| **Very Strong** | Above-average ease in using numbers to an advantage; creative use of approximation and rules of thumb |

---

## Dimension 3 — Ability to Identify Possible Errors

Self-awareness that you'll be able to check yourself on the job. Scores whether you can pinpoint where your answer is wrong and why.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to discuss possible errors |
| **Weak** | Failed to reasonably justify decisions when pressed and/or made bad judgment calls |
| **Neutral** | Correctly identified some possible mistakes but missed others |
| **Strong** | Correctly identified possible errors and suggested ways to increase accuracy with more information |
| **Very Strong** | Possible errors covered thoroughly; alternatives were offered; pros/cons were neatly summarized |

---

## Dimension 4 — Communication

All interviews assess communication skills. Scores clarity and consistency of your thought process throughout the session.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to communicate clearly despite repeated prompts |
| **Weak** | Poor communication throughout; interviewer had trouble following despite prompts |
| **Neutral** | Communication varied — clear in some areas but vague/incomplete in others |
| **Strong** | Good communication skills; articulated thought process clearly and consistently |
| **Very Strong** | Clear, proactive communication; anticipated questions, articulated reasons for decisions, "checked in" throughout |

---

## Dimension 5 — Collaboration

You're expected to lead the discussion, but interviews are opportunities to collaborate. Scores how well you involve your interviewer in the process.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to take the lead; didn't respond to guidance |
| **Weak** | Struggled to stay on track without guidance |
| **Neutral** | Took the lead and performed well, but may have needed redirects or hints |
| **Strong** | Effectively led the discussion; involved the interviewer throughout |
| **Very Strong** | Took the lead and made exceptional use of the interviewer; discussion was more collaboration than interview |

---

## Dimension 6 — Creativity

FAANG companies and startups look for creative and enthusiastic problem-solvers. Estimation questions are opportunities to show novel thinking while maintaining logical rigor.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Very Weak / Missing** | Failed to show enthusiasm or creative thinking |
| **Weak** | Solutions were bland; didn't show interest in the problem |
| **Neutral** | Showed reasonable insight, but nothing exceptional |
| **Strong** | Thought process was logical but creative |
| **Very Strong** | Exceptionally creative and out-of-the-box thinking on display; logic maintained, novel solutions found |

---

## Master Summary

| Dimension | Category | What Gets You "Very Strong" |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem-Solving** | Critical Thinking | Insightful questions that set up a clean, accurate calculation |
| **Comfort with Numbers** | Critical Thinking | Creative use of approximation and rules of thumb, feels effortless |
| **Identify Possible Errors** | Critical Thinking | Thorough error analysis with alternatives and clearly summarized pros/cons |
| **Communication** | Culture Fit | Proactive, anticipates questions, checks in throughout |
| **Collaboration** | Culture Fit | Session feels like collaboration, not a one-way performance |
| **Creativity** | Culture Fit | Novel and logical simultaneously — something the interviewer hadn't considered |
