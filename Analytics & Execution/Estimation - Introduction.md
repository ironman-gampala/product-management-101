# Introduction to Estimation Questions

> As a Product Manager, you'll need to make decisions based on incomplete information — quickly gauging market size, doing a gut-check on user interest in a feature, or sizing an opportunity. Estimation questions assess your ability to do this.

---

## What Is an Estimation Question?

Estimation questions like *"How many rental properties are listed on Airbnb in San Francisco?"* can seem impossible at first. The key is realizing that **interviewers don't expect you to arrive at the exact answer**. They're interested in how you solve problems.

**Example questions:**
- *"How much money does the Google Play Store make in a year?"*
- *"Estimate the number of videos watched on YouTube per day."*
- *"What will the market size be for driverless cars in 2025?"*

---

## What to Expect

Most estimation questions can be answered using the same process, though the information and intuitions you draw on will change. You might see estimation questions that incorporate:

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Strategic understanding** | "What's the TAM of Slack?" |
| **Technical knowledge** | "How much does it cost to run YouTube for a day?" |
| **Execution** | "How much would it cost to capture Street View images for Manhattan?" |

**Typical interview format:**
- Duration: 40 minutes to an hour
- Some interviewers ask 2–3 questions with little follow-up
- Others ask a single question and spend 15 minutes diving deeper

---

## What Interviewers Are Looking For

Interviewers want to see you:

- Make sense of the situation
- Break down the problem
- Make reasonable assumptions
- Incorporate edge cases and discard unimportant factors
- Manipulate numbers
- Evaluate your answer in context

---

## Okay vs. Good vs. Great Answers

| Level | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Okay** | Provides a reasonable breakdown and makes large but reasonable assumptions for estimated values. The candidate may feel their final answer is off, but the assumptions are too broad to fully diagnose why. |
| **Good** | Breaks down the problem into its critical components. Estimated values are well-justified. The candidate can reason about why their answer makes sense and can examine how different assumptions would have changed the final answer. |
| **Great** | Clear breakdown, thorough without needless complication, incorporating the larger context. Estimated values feel tight — as if no other value would make sense given the assumptions. The candidate can discuss the meaning of the final answer in context, entertain different assumptions, and assess how these would affect the answer. |

---

## How to Prepare

**Practice is critical.** You'll build confidence in your ability to break down problems and estimate unknowns over time.

**Memorize benchmark facts** that could reasonably come up in estimation interviews. These give you anchors to jump-start your process and ground your answer in reality:

| Benchmark | Approximate Value |
|---|---|
| World population | ~8 billion |
| US population | ~330 million |
| Average global life expectancy | ~73 years |
| US average household size | ~2.5 people |
| US average annual income | ~$60,000 |
| US smartphone penetration | ~85% |
| Global internet users | ~5 billion |

> **Tip:** A few figures like these in your back pocket can help jump-start your process and ground your answer in reality when you're drawing a blank.
