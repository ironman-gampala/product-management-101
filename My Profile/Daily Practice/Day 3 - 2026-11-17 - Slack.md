## Model turn: the close (about 60 seconds)

> *"Let me close with what I'd ship and how I'd know it's working.*
>
> *The **prerequisite** is trust in the ranking. Before AI ranks anything, it has to get the top five right at
> least 9 times out of 10, tested against what people actually opened first. Until then, **v1 uses simple
> rules**: incidents and DMs from senior leaders first, the user's own rules second. I'd start with
> engineering power users at a few large customers, with Jira, GitHub and PagerDuty.*
>
> ***What waits:** AI ranking, until it passes that test. The richer cross-thread summary, until people trust
> the panel. Video calls are out of scope, since Huddles exists.*
>
> *My **north star** stays what's on the board: the median time it takes a power user to respond to a message
> that needs them. It measures whether they stop missing important things, not whether they open our panel.*
>
> *For **guardrails**, the risk is the messages we rank low. If teammates get slower replies, they'll mark
> everything urgent and the noise comes back. So I'd watch reply times for low-ranked messages, and how often
> someone moves a low-ranked item to the top.*
>
> ***To summarise:** power users miss critical messages because Slack treats every notification the same. A
> priority panel, starting with rules and moving to AI once it's proven, shows them what needs them first.
> That's the productivity that helps Slack survive the renewal review against Teams."*

| Move | Why it scores |
|---|---|
| A prerequisite with a number (9 in 10) | Something that must be true, not a launch date |
| "v1 uses simple rules" | Names the cut as a decision |
| What waits, with reasons | The senior signal |
| "Stays what's on the board" | No drift to usage numbers |
| A guardrail on low-ranked messages | Could really get worse as the NSM improves; also the second-order effect |
| Summary ties back to Teams and renewals | Closes the loop with the strategy step |
