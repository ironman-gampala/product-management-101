## How it went

My content caught up. I described a vivid, timed day for a busy lead and found a strong insight: they have
**four free hours a day, but scattered in 30-minute gaps**. Under challenge, I reframed the persona to the
**player-coach lead** (does IC work and attends many meetings), which was my best moment. I named three
numbered, different ideas: focus blocks, organizer suggestions and "send me the summary". The stacking
example was concrete (4, 5 and 6 pm calls → free 5:30–7).

**What cost points was landing.** I asked for time or announced the next step four times, then paused. I
never said a problem statement with "because", and I picked two problems. My north star drifted from 3 hours
on the board to 2 hours in the metrics step. Idea 1 (Focus time) already exists. The MVP had no
prerequisite, no v1 scope and no cuts, and I ended with "and also, if yeah".

## Model turn: MVP and close (about 60 seconds)

> *"Let me close with what I'd ship first.*
>
> *The **prerequisite** is that our suggestions must be right. If we suggest a time that doesn't work for
> everyone, organizers stop trusting it after one bad suggestion. So before launch, I'd check that at least
> 9 out of 10 suggested times are truly free for every attendee.*
>
> *For **v1**, I'd ship organizer suggestions only while a meeting is being booked, only for meetings inside
> one company, starting with a few Workspace customers whose teams have heavy meeting loads.*
>
> ***What waits:** re-suggesting when meetings get cancelled, because it messages everyone and needs more
> trust first; 'send me the summary', because it depends on meeting notes being good enough; and I'd build on
> the existing Focus time rather than rebuild it.*
>
> *My **north star** stays what I wrote on the board: the share of users with three or more hours of focus
> time a week, in blocks of two hours or more. **Guardrails:** total meeting time, so we're stacking meetings
> and not moving them into the evening, and how often organizers dismiss the suggestion.*
>
> ***To summarise:** player-coach leads have four free hours a day, but they're scattered in 30-minute gaps,
> because meetings land anywhere. Calendar can suggest better times while meetings are booked, and turn those
> gaps into real focus blocks."*

| Move | Why it scores |
|---|---|
| A prerequisite that must be **true** (9 in 10) | Not a timeline |
| A narrow v1 (internal meetings, a few customers) | Shows I can cut scope |
| "What waits", with a reason for each | Cutting with reasons is the senior signal |
| "Stays what I wrote on the board" | No 3h → 2h drift |
| "Not moving them into the evening" | A real guardrail and a second-order effect |
| "To summarise…" | A strong last memory instead of "if yeah" |
