## What to improve
1. **Go wide before deep.** Say 3 numbered one-liners before detailing any idea.
2. **Walk the whole flow.** Entry → screen → action → behind the scenes → result → edge case.
3. **Stay consistent.** Booking depends on find parking, so it can't be in v1.
4. **A prerequisite is what must be true** (data accuracy), not where I launch (top 5 cities).
5. **Finish the close:** what waits, guardrails, and "To summarise…".
6. **Lock in decisions:** pick the market myself; say "I'll assume", not "I hope".

## Where I stand today

## How it went

The first full timed mock. I asked a strong "why is Google building this now?" question and came up with a
smart success signal: no circling near the destination. But I froze at users (found one) and at solutions
(found one), and needed the lens tools each time. I had three solution ideas without noticing, because I
never numbered them. The flow stopped at the screen. In the MVP I put booking into v1, even though I had
argued earlier that booking depends on find parking.

**Big insight:** the problem isn't a lack of ideas. I go deep on the first idea straight away, and that
blocks everything else. Every freeze started that way.

### What went wrong in the MVP
1. **Booking in v1 contradicted my own pick.**
2. **"Top 5 cities" is launch scope, not a prerequisite.** A prerequisite is what must be *true*: is our
   "usually free" data accurate enough?
3. **No "what waits."** Cutting is the senior signal.
4. **No guardrails** (shown-free-but-full rate, door-to-door time).
5. **No closing summary.**

## Model turn: the MVP (about 1 minute)

> *"**Prerequisite:** our 'usually free' labels must be accurate. Before launch, I'd check them against
> partner lot counts and test drives, and only launch where we're right at least 8 times out of 10. If we
> say 'free' and it's full, people stop trusting the feature.*
>
> ***v1:** find parking only, in the 50 hardest-to-park areas in each of the top five cities: malls,
> restaurant streets and markets. It has the 'parking is hard here' tag, the list with availability labels,
> navigation to the lot entrance, and saving where you parked.*
>
> ***What waits:** booking, because it needs payments and contracts with each lot; I'd pilot it in month
> five or six. Automatic re-routing, because in v1 we only suggest the next nearest lot. Two-wheelers,
> because their parking is very different.*
>
> ***Metrics:** my north star is the share of trips to hard areas where people park within 3 minutes
> without circling. My guardrails are how often we said 'free' and it was full, and total door-to-door time.*
>
> ***To summarise:** for leisure drivers going somewhere new, the real problem is that they can't see where
> parking is or whether it's free. Find parking fixes that first, and builds the base for booking next."*
