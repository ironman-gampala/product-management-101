2. **Not a guardrail:** "people finishing without using Ask AI" is feature adoption flipped around.

5. **Asking permission instead of stating:** "Can I take these as assumptions?", "if we are good to go", "if this looks good for you".
6. Problem statement: one sentence for the first time, but no "because", and only after three asks.

## How it went

I started instantly with "I assume" (global, two quarters) and asked why. That got me the why-now: learners
drift into Shorts, AI assistants answer "how do I" questions, and Ask AI, Courses and chapters already exist.
I only asked one real question again, and didn't scope what "learning" means.

In strategy, my mission and why-now were generic ("tons of videos"), and I didn't use the two why-nows I'd
been handed. The north star turn was my cleanest: three options (enrolments, weekly active users, 80%
completion), and I picked completion because the problem is drifting. When pushed ("I could play it in the
background"), I moved to passing the end assessment, compared to the start. I said "finished" at first; the
interviewer had to fix it to "passed".

Users were one-liners for the first time in three mocks: students and early professionals vs. casual
explorers. I picked "active learners" with one reason. There were only 2 segments, and the one I picked was
really two people.

Pain points: a concrete LLM example and a journey walk, with three pains hidden in one (drift mid-session, no
return, no sense of progress). The interviewer asked "why?" and then "one line?" before I gave a one-sentence
problem statement, without a "because". I also said "a sense of entitlement" when I meant achievement.

Solutions: I took 30 seconds, then asked permission to assume Ask AI and Courses exist. After a push I gave two
ideas, both in full: AI explainers at the hard parts, and plan my program (schedule, reminders, cohort
progress). I called prerequisites "edge cases". When asked to pick one, I chose AI explainers, which didn't fit
my board problem ("no plan"). The guardrail was "people finishing without using Ask AI". I asked what the
interviewer meant by the connection question, got a coach explanation, then added in-video questions and
summarised, ending on the north star.

**Big insight: my problem statement and my solution were built separately.** I picked the solution from a
personal hunch ("confusion kills motivation"), not from the "because" on the board. The fix: before picking,
read the "because" out loud and ask "which idea kills this?". Pick that one, or update the board openly.

## Model turn: solutions, tied to the board (about 60 seconds)

> *"My board says learners quit because a playlist has no plan, no checkpoints and no reward. So I'll take one
> idea for each gap.*
>
> *One, for no plan: **plan my program**. You tell YouTube your goal and your free time, and it turns the
> playlist into a schedule with reminders. It builds on Courses.*
>
> *Two, for no checkpoints: **in-video skill checks**. A short question after each chapter, written by Ask AI
> from the transcript, so you know where you stand.*
>
> *Three, for no reward: a **learning cohort**. You see others who started the same week, and where you stand
> among them.*
>
> *I'll pick plan my program, with the skill checks built in. Two reasons. It attacks the main gap, which is no
> plan. And every plan ends in the skill check, so it moves the north star directly. I'm not picking the cohort
> first, because it only works once a course has enough people starting together, and most playlists don't.*
>
> *AI explainers at the hard parts are a good idea, but they fix confusion, which isn't what my board says is
> causing the drop-off. I'd test that in v2."*

| Move | Why it scores |
|---|---|
| Starts by reading the "because" from the board | Every idea is visibly tied to the root cause |
| One idea per gap, one line each | Wide before deep; each idea is numbered |
| Pick with 2 reasons, one linked to the north star | The pick moves the number on the board |
| A reject with a dependency reason (cohort needs scale) | Rule #1: decide, reason, reject |
| Names the off-problem idea and parks it honestly | Consistency (#6) without throwing away a good idea |

## A real guardrail I could have said

> *"My guardrail is accuracy. If Ask AI writes wrong explanations or wrong quiz answers, pass rates can go up
> while people learn something false. So I'd sample explanations for expert review and track the error rate.
> I'd also watch creator watch time, because if learners skip to the summary, creators earn less and may stop
> making long tutorials."*
