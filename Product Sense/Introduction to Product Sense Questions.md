# Introduction to Product Sense Questions

---

## What Is Product Sense?

Product sense is about understanding what makes a product good and making sound judgments about what is worth building. At its core, it comes down to four things:

- **User sense** — deeply understanding who you're building for and why they care
- **Market sense** — knowing where a product sits competitively and strategically
- **Creative thinking** — generating ideas that are genuinely distinct, not just variations of the obvious
- **A developed Point of View** — an earned opinion on what *"good"* actually looks like

---

## What to Expect

Product sense interviews are sometimes called **"Product Design"** or **"Product Thinking."** These rounds ask questions about designing, improving, or discussing products. Questions fall into five categories:

### 1. Design a New Product
Given a brief, build something from scratch.
- *"Design a parking solution for one of your favourite navigation apps."* — Meta
- *"Design a communication app for children."* — Stripe, LinkedIn, Roblox

### 2. Improve an Existing Product
Make a real product better by starting with users and their pain points.
- *"How would you improve YouTube's recommendation algorithm?"* — Google, ADP, Meta
- *"How would you improve the Amazon shopping experience?"* — Amazon

### 3. Novel Tech Framing
Given a technology with no established playbook, decide what to build.
- *"You have text-to-music capabilities. How would you productize it?"* — OpenAI
- *"You have invented a memory machine. Go to market."* — OpenAI

### 4. Favorite Product
Name your favorite product and either improve it or build a competitor for it.
- *"What's your favorite product and why?"* — Robinhood, Stripe, Google, and 33+ more

### 5. Live Prototyping
Build a working prototype during the interview using AI tools. Meta introduced this format; Google, OpenAI, and others are signaling similar moves.

---

## How Product Sense Interviews Have Changed

**Two shifts have made these interviews meaningfully different from a few years ago.**

**1. Product sense matters more now.**
AI has made building cheaper, and the bottleneck has shifted from execution to judgment. Since shipping is easy, the new critical skillset is *"What do we ship and why?"* AI also makes it easy to generate product ideas — which is exactly why the bar for what interviewers expect has risen. A list of ideas is no longer the signal. A genuine point of view is.

**2. Prototyping can now be part of the interview.**
One of the biggest structural changes in recent PM interview transcripts: Meta introduced a dedicated **AI Product Sense round** — 30 minutes of traditional product sense, then candidates are given an internal AI tool and asked to vibe-code a working prototype live.

> **Verified — L5 Meta PM candidate:**
> *"That AI round was the unusual part: about 30 minutes of normal product sense and then a switch into Meta's Llama tool to vibe code a prototype live."*

If early adopters (Meta, DeepMind, and Frontier AI labs) find success with live prototyping rounds, many companies will follow.

## What Interviewers Are Looking For

Interviewers ask product sense questions to assess your ability to:

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Strategic connection** | Connect your product ideas to the company's mission, business model, and competitive position — not just to what users might want |
| **User specificity** | Think from a specific user's perspective, with pain points that come from their real experience rather than a generic template |
| **Idea quality** | Generate ideas that are meaningfully distinct from each other, grounded in the root problem, with at least one you're genuinely excited about |
| **Concreteness** | Translate a concept into something tangible; narrate design decisions as you go; handle implementation follow-ups with composure |
| **Conversational delivery** | Bring the interviewer along step by step — treat the interview as a conversation, not a performance |

---

## Leveling: What Changes at Senior+

The five signals are consistent across levels. What changes is **how deep each one goes**.

### Solid Answer
Executes all five signals competently:
- A named user segment with a genuine pain point
- A strategy section that touches the company mission and picks a north star metric
- Three distinct ideas
- A prototype that gets the core idea across
- A clear, easy-to-follow answer throughout

### Senior+ Answer
The same framework produces a meaningfully different quality:

| Signal | Senior+ Version |
|---|---|
| **User segment** | Specific to what *this technology or company* enables — not a generic cut that could apply to any product |
| **Strategy section** | Connects the product to the company's mission and competitive position *before* the interviewer asks for it |
| **Ideas** | Meaningfully different from each other; the winning choice is argued against the north star established earlier — not just *"it's the most impactful"* |
| **Prototype** | Narrates every design decision; treats implementation follow-ups as a natural extension of the answer, not a separate test |
| **Overall arc** | Includes a longer vision beyond the MVP; names what you are *not* building and exactly why |

**The clearest markers of a senior+ answer:**
- Unprompted strategic framing
- Teaching the interviewer something they didn't know previously
- Judgment calls that go beyond the framework — naming what you're *not* building and exactly why

> **The most consistent differentiator for Senior+ PMs across all companies in our research:** It was not framework execution. It was whether the candidate had a **genuine point of view** — on strategy, on which user mattered most and why, on which idea was actually worth building.
