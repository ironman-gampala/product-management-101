# How to Answer the "Favorite Product" Question

> *"What's your favorite product?"* is one of the most commonly asked questions in PM interviews. It's a simple question that serves multiple purposes: a gentle introduction to the interview, a window into how you think, and an opportunity for the interviewer to get to know you.

---

## What to Expect

Some variant of *"What's your favorite product?"* is often the **first real question** you'll get. Think of it as a warm-up — it's meant to be short.

| Phase | Time |
|---|---|
| Your answer + short explanation | 3–7 minutes |
| Follow-up discussion | 3–5 minutes |

The interviewer is usually looking for you to answer **as a user** — but it's possible they want your perspective as a PM analyzing the product. Read the room.

---

## Common Variants

Some interviewers add conditions to avoid canned answers:

- *"What's your favorite Google product?"* (in a Google interview)
- *"What's your favorite non-tech product?"*
- *"What's your favorite app?"*
- *"What are your three favorite products?"*

Some ask for a different angle:

- *"What's a product you hate that others love?"*
- *"What's a product that's interesting but hasn't reached its full potential?"*

---

## How to Choose a Product

Choose something you're **genuinely interested in** and familiar enough with to speak about in depth. The product should let you demonstrate what we call the **Three P's**:

| P | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Passion** | You actually care about this product and use it regularly |
| **Perspective** | You have a nuanced opinion — not just *"it's well-designed"* |
| **Personality** | The choice reveals something about who you are as a PM |

### Addressing common concerns

- **Popular products are fine** — as long as you have an opinion that goes beyond the obvious
- **Obscure products are fine** — but be ready to explain how the product works since the interviewer may not know it
- **You don't have to choose the interviewing company's product**

### What to avoid

- **Very simple products** — a number two pencil is hard to fill ten minutes on
- **Products from a direct competitor** — not forbidden, but telling a Lyft PM that Uber is your favorite product is a risk worth avoiding

---

## The 3-Step Framework

### Step 1 — Name and Explain the Product

Tell the interviewer what your favorite product is and briefly describe it.

- If it's popular, a sentence or two is enough — but mention any specific aspects that are important to *you*
- If it's obscure, spend a few sentences on what the product does and why it exists

*Continuing example: Notion*

### Step 2 — Describe How the Product Fits Your Needs

Paint a vivid picture. Answer these questions naturally:
- What do you care about?
- Why do you use this product?
- What specific design decisions does the product make that serve your needs?

**Don't just describe the product's features — describe the choices designers made that support your specific needs.**

> **Weak:** *"I like Gmail because it stores my emails."*
>
> **Strong:** *"Gmail's label and filter system lets me build a personal triage workflow — I process my inbox once a day, and everything else is auto-sorted. The keyboard shortcuts mean I can go from inbox zero to done without touching my mouse."*

> **Example — Notion:**
>
> *"Personally, I try to be pretty organized. I like to make a table of books I'm reading and a notes doc for each book to track my thoughts as I read. Notion makes it really easy to create and link these different document formats — and importantly, it lets me keep relevant information connected regardless of format. I used to use Google Drive, but I always felt like a Google Doc or Sheet is meant to be a standalone container. Notion is built around the idea that information should be relational, not siloed, and that matches how I actually think."*

### Step 3 — Handle Follow-Up Questions

Expect the interviewer to take the conversation somewhere after your answer. Common directions:

| Follow-up Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Analytical** | *"How would you measure the success of this product?"* |
| **Product** | *"How would Google build a smart version of this?"* |
| **Strategy** | *"If you were CEO of the company, how would you grow this product?"* |
| **Improvement** *(most common)* | *"How would you change this product?"* |

**Prepare your "how would you change it" answer in advance.** Identify a specific pain point, and explain how you'd want it fixed.

> **Example — changing Notion:**
>
> *"One annoying thing with Notion is that it's difficult to index information across different pages — search doesn't work well across a workspace. It would be great if there was a way to automatically collect information together using hashtags. If a few different book notes each had a good point about the economy, I could tag that note #econ, and then view all tagged results together on one page. It keeps the relational philosophy intact but adds a lightweight cross-page retrieval layer that Notion currently lacks."*

## Quick Reference

```
PICK YOUR PRODUCT
→ Passion: you actually use and care about it
→ Perspective: you have an opinion beyond "it's well-designed"
→ Personality: it reveals something about you as a PM

DELIVER YOUR ANSWER (3–7 min)
1. Name it + one-sentence description
2. Describe your needs and how the product serves them
   → Focus on design decisions, not just features
3. Have a "how would you change it" answer ready

FOLLOW-UP PREP
→ How would you measure success?
→ How would you improve it?
→ How would a competitor build this?
```
