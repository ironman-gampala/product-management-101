# Product Strategy — Senior (SNR) and Senior+ (SNR+) Bar

Strategy questions ask **what a company should do**: build next, launch, grow, price, enter a market,
or respond to a competitor. This is where the level is decided most clearly. At Senior and above,
*"What should Airbnb build?"* replaces *"Build onboarding for Airbnb Experiences."*

## Sample questions

| # | Question | Subtype | Good practice for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You're the CPO of Zoom, facing strong competition from Teams. What would you do?" | Competitive response | Any |
| 2 | "What should Okta build in response to AI agents needing access to company apps?" | What to build next | Okta |
| 3 | "How would you 10x Duolingo?" | Growth | Any |
| 4 | "Rippling has strong payroll sign-ups but low use of its IT product among the same customers. What would you do?" | Growth / cross-sell (B2B) | Rippling |
| 5 | "Should Google enter the grocery market, and how?" | Market entry | Any |
| 6 | "How would you price a new AI copilot for Eightfold's recruiters?" | Pricing | Eightfold AI |
| 7 | "Imagine OpenAI built a ChatGPT pod for hotels and offices. Take it to market." | Go-to-market | AI-first companies |
| 8 | "What's the biggest threat to YouTube?" | Strategic analysis | Any |

## The level ladder

```
  PM2 / L4   →  Lists options with pros and cons. Ends with "it depends" or a safe, generic pick.

  SNR / L5   →  Reads the business model correctly, names what changed, filters options with
                principles, makes ONE specific call and defends it when pushed.

  SNR+ / L6  →  Finds the tension between what the company says and what it actually optimises for.
                Explains why now is different from 18 months ago. Names the strongest counter-argument
                unprompted and the signal that would change the call. Thinks like a general manager:
                sequencing, go-to-market, pricing, org and partners.
```

## Time split

| Level | Business goals + landscape | Options + recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| PM2 | About 5 minutes | Most of the time, often as a list |
| SNR | About 10 minutes | About 15 minutes, ending in one call |
| SNR+ | About **half** the interview | The call feels obvious because the analysis earned it |

## Step by step: what to say

| Step | SNR (must say) | SNR+ (add this) | Example line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarify** | Pin the time horizon, the success metric and the constraints. State your assumptions. | Reframe the question if the stated one is the wrong one. | "I'll assume a 3-year horizon, and that success means net revenue retention, not just new logos." |
| **Business goals** | Say how the company actually makes money and what it is optimising for right now. | Name the tension between the stated goal and real behaviour, and use it to filter options. | "Zoom says it's a platform, but 80% of revenue still comes from meetings seats. That tension decides what we do." |
| **Landscape** | Name real competitors, their position, and the shift that makes this question matter now. | Explain how the dynamics are moving, and why now is different from 18 months ago. | "Eighteen months ago, agents didn't sign in to anything. Now every agent needs an identity, and nobody owns that yet." |
| **Guiding principles** | Give 2–3 principles. Each one must **rule out** at least one option. | Principles come from the company's unique strengths, not generic values. | "Principle one: build where our identity graph is the advantage. That rules out building our own agent." |
| **Options** | Brainstorm widely first, then filter with the principles to a shortlist of 3 or fewer. | Include one option that sounds wrong at first but survives the filter. | "The surprising option is to give the agent identity layer away free, to become the standard." |
| **Recommendation** | Make one clear, specific call, and explain why it beats the runner-up. | Make it specific enough that it could be wrong, and say so. | "I'd build agent identity as a new product line, sold to existing workforce customers first." |
| **Counter-argument** | Defend the call when the interviewer pushes back. | Raise the strongest counter-argument **yourself**, then say why you still land there. | "The strongest case against this is that Microsoft bundles it. I still land here because…" |
| **Sequencing** | Say what happens first, next and later (0–6 months, 6–18 months, 18+ months). | Tie each phase to a milestone that must be true before the next one starts. | "Phase two starts only when 50 design partners have agents in production." |
| **Metrics + reversal** | Give one success metric and one guardrail. | Name the exact signal that would make you change course. | "If fewer than 20% of pilot customers expand after 2 quarters, I'd stop and partner instead." |
| **Close** | Summarise the call, the why and the first step. | Cover go-to-market, pricing, partners and the team you'd need. | "I'd start with our 100 largest customers, priced per agent, and a team of two pods." |

## Checklist

**SNR — say all of these:**
1. The company's real revenue model and current priority.
2. A specific "what changed" for why now.
3. Principles that rule out options.
4. A shortlist of 3 or fewer, after filtering.
5. **One** specific recommendation, never "it depends".
6. Why it beats the runner-up.
7. A defence when pushed.
8. A sequence: now, next, later.
9. A success metric and a guardrail.

**SNR+ — add these:**

10. The tension between stated goals and actual behaviour.
11. How the market is moving, not just who is in it.
12. The strongest counter-argument, raised unprompted.
13. The signal that would make you reverse.
14. General manager thinking: go-to-market, pricing, partners, org.

---

## Red flags

| Red flag | Why it costs you |
|---|---|
| Ending with "it depends" | No decision means no level signal. |
| A mission statement used as business strategy | It shows you don't know how the company makes money. |
| "It's a big market" as why now | Timing beats market size. |
| Principles that don't eliminate anything | They are decoration, not filters. |
| Dropping the call as soon as you're challenged | Seniors defend, then adjust only with a reason. |
| Strategy that ends when the feature ships | Senior+ includes go-to-market and sequencing. |
