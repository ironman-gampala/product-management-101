# Leadership and Drive — Senior (SNR) and Senior+ (SNR+) Bar

Behavioural rounds ask you to **tell a real story** ("Tell me about a time…"). They also include the
**experience deep dive** ("Walk me through a product you built"), **"Tell me about yourself"** and
**"Why this company?"**. The level is decided mostly by the **scope** of your stories and the
**decisions you personally made**.

## Sample questions

| # | Question | Subtype | Good practice for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tell me about yourself." | Intro | Every loop |
| 2 | "Walk me through the most impactful product you've built." | Experience deep dive | Every loop (95%) |
| 3 | "Tell me about a time you strongly disagreed with your manager." | Conflict / disagree and commit | Any |
| 4 | "Tell me about a time you influenced a team you had no authority over." | Influence | Okta, Rippling |
| 5 | "Tell me about a product or launch that failed." | Failure | Any |
| 6 | "Tell me about a time you made a decision without all the information." | Ambiguity / bias for action | Rippling (speed) |
| 7 | "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your area." | Ownership | Any |
| 8 | "Why Okta?" / "Why Rippling?" / "Why Eightfold?" | Why this company | Every loop |

## The level ladder

```
  PM2 / L4   →  Stories about a feature or a task. "We did…". Stakeholders are named but nobody
                disagrees. The result is a metric with no reflection.

  SNR / L5   →  Stories at TEAM level: you owned the roadmap or a whole problem. Named stakeholders
                with real disagreement, a visible decision with the alternative you rejected,
                a number in the result, and a clear "I".

  SNR+ / L6  →  Stories at ORG level or across teams. You found the problem nobody assigned, took a
                real risk to your time or credibility, changed how other teams work, and close with
                an insight that applies beyond the project.
```

## Step by step: STAR+ at each level

| Step | SNR (must say) | SNR+ (add this) | Example line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook (first line)** | Open with the stakes in one sentence. | Make the stakes specific enough that the rest sounds impressive by itself. | "A bank was about to miss its card launch date, and the delay would cost us the contract." |
| **Situation** | Give context in 2–3 sentences, with a number for scale. | Show the scope: how many teams, which leaders, why it mattered beyond your team. | "It touched 6 teams across issuance, risk and the bank's own IT." |
| **Task** | Say what *you* owned. | Say what you took on that was *not* your job, and why. | "Nobody owned the cut-over plan, so I took it, even though it wasn't my area." |
| **Action** | Use "I", not "we". Give 2–3 actions, including **one decision with the alternative you rejected**. | Show the tension: who disagreed, what they were protecting, and why you chose this approach over another. | "The risk lead wanted a full freeze. I proposed a phased switch instead, because a freeze would miss the date." |
| **Result** | Give a number and a date. | Show what changed beyond the project: a process, a team, a company decision. | "We launched 2 days early, and the phased switch became the default for every bank after that." |
| **Learning (+)** | Say what you'd do differently. | Give an insight that transfers to other situations. | "Now I agree the rollback plan before the launch plan. It turns risk teams into partners." |

## Special formats

| Format | SNR | SNR+ |
|---|---|---|
| **Tell me about yourself** (about 90 seconds) | Past → present → why this role, with one flagship result. | One clear theme that links your past to this company's problem. |
| **Experience deep dive** | Know every number, decision and trade-off. Answer "why" three levels deep. | Explain the strategy behind the product, what you'd change now, and how it affected the business. |
| **Why this company** | Name a specific product, problem or bet, and link it to your experience. | Show a point of view on where the company is going and what you'd do in your first 90 days. |
| **Failure story** | A real failure with real cost, your part in it, and what changed. | A failure from a bold bet, how you caught it, and the system you built so it doesn't repeat. |

## Checklist

**SNR — make sure every story has:**
1. A one-line hook with the stakes.
2. Team-level scope, with a number for scale.
3. "I", not "we".
4. A named stakeholder and a real disagreement.
5. One decision, plus the alternative you rejected.
6. A result with a number.
7. A learning.
8. Two minutes for the first telling, then go deeper on follow-ups.

**SNR+ — add these:**

9. Org-level or cross-team scope.
10. Something you took on that wasn't your job, and the risk you accepted.
11. Why the other person disagreed, in their terms.
12. A change that lasted beyond the project.
13. An insight that transfers.

**Story bank:** have 6–8 stories that cover conflict, failure, influence, ambiguity, ownership, a big
launch, a data-driven decision and a people story. One story can answer 2–3 questions.

---

## Red flags

| Red flag | Why it costs you |
|---|---|
| "We did…" all the way through | The interviewer can't see your part. |
| Everyone agreed | No tension means no judgement is shown. |
| A story about a small task | The scope sets the level. |
| No number in the result | It sounds like it didn't matter. |
| Blaming others in the failure story | It signals low ownership. |
| A memorised script | Follow-ups expose it quickly. |
| A generic "why this company" | It sounds like you'd say it anywhere. |
