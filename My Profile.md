## 2. Target roles

| Company | Level | What they do | What they will likely test | Prep focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rippling** | PM | One platform for HR, IT and finance (payroll, benefits, devices, apps, spend), all built on one employee record | Product sense (B2B), execution and metrics, speed and ownership | How one employee record powers many products; admin workflows; SMB vs mid-market buyers |
| **Eightfold AI** | PM2 (≈ L4) | AI talent platform: hiring, internal mobility, skills matching for large enterprises | Product sense with AI, metrics for AI quality, trust | Measuring match quality, bias and fairness in hiring AI, human-in-the-loop, enterprise buyers |
| **Okta** | Senior PM (≈ L5) | Identity: workforce identity (employees signing in to work apps) and customer identity (Auth0, for developers) | B2B product sense, strategy, technical depth, leadership scope | SSO, MFA, passwordless, admin vs end-user vs developer personas, B2B metrics |

**The pattern: all three are B2B enterprise SaaS, and all three are about the workforce** (people, their jobs, their access). Rippling even overlaps with Okta on identity and app access. This changes how I should practise:
- **Shift most drills from consumer questions (like Google Maps) to B2B questions.**
- In B2B, the **buyer** (CHRO, CIO, CFO), the **admin** (HR or IT ops), and the **end user** (employee, recruiter, developer) are different people. Every answer should name all three, then pick one.
- B2B metrics are different: activation, time to value, seats, retention, expansion, admin hours saved.
- My Zeta background (B2B platform, trust-heavy, regulated) is directly relevant. Use it in stories.

**Level gap to be aware of:** PM2 and Senior PM are different bars. The repo's leveling guide (`Intro/Understanding Leveling.md`) says the jump from L4 to L5 is bigger than from L3 to L4, and the strategy section of product sense is where the level gets decided.

## 4. Weak spots (observed 2026-11-14)

| # | Weak spot | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Freezing in a 30-minute interview** | I stall at users and solutions and say "I can't think of more" | Use the time budget + lenses (section 6) |
| 2 | **Getting stuck on one solution** | I go deep on the first idea straight away, which blocks other ideas | Go wide first: 3 one-line ideas, then pick, then detail |
| 3 | **Not walking the solution flow** | I describe entry + screen, then stop | Always say all 6: entry → screen → action → behind the scenes → result → edge case |
| 4 | **Mixing steps** | I describe pain points while listing users | One line per user; save the story for pain points |
| 5 | **Not locking in decisions** | My north star was described, never named. The market wasn't picked until asked | Say it in one sentence and write it on the board |
| 6 | **Contradicting my own pick** | I said booking depends on find parking, then put booking in v1 | Re-read the board before the MVP step |
| 7 | **Guardrail confusion** | I used the north star flipped around as a guardrail | Guardrail = what could get worse while the north star improves |
| 8 | **Filler and weak endings** | "uh, uh," "so yeah," "I hope it would be" | Start each step with a planned first word; end with a closing line |
| 9 | **Asking for time / announcing instead of starting** *(added 2026-11-16)* | "Let me take 2 minutes…", "I'll move to the next one…" then silence — 4× in one mock | Think out loud with the lens questions; the first sentence of the step comes out within 3 seconds |
| 10 | **Not landing the decision** *(added 2026-11-16)* | Describes well, but the step ends without "so my ___ is ___ because ___" (no problem statement, two picks, NSM drift) | End every step with one landing sentence and write it on the board |

**Root cause (one line):** under time pressure I search for an *answer* instead of following a *route*. The ideas are there, but the habits aren't automatic yet.

## 6. My route (memorise this)

```
  0–3    CLARIFY        3–4 questions in ONE batch → pick market → "I'll assume…"
  3–6    STRATEGY       mission → why this company → NSM in one sentence (write it down)
  6–11   USERS          2 lenses (why + how often) → 4 one-liners → pick 1 + why not runner-up
  11–16  PAIN POINTS    goal → walk the journey → 3 pains → pick 1 → problem statement with "because"
  16–24  SOLUTIONS      3 lenses (information / automation / partner-social) → 3 one-liners
                        → pick 1 → 6-step flow
  24–28  MVP + METRICS  prerequisite → v1 → what waits → NSM + 2 guardrails
  28–30  SUMMARY        "To summarise…"
```

**Transition lines:**
- "Before I dive in, I'd like to ask a few quick questions."
- "Let me split users by why they use it and how often."
- "Let me walk through their journey and see where it goes wrong."
- "Let me first name three different approaches, then pick one."
- "Let me walk through how it works, from entry to result."
- "Before launch, the one thing that has to be true is…"
- "To summarise…"

## 7. Three-week plan

### Week 1: build the habits (15–20 min a day)
- **Drill A, users in 60 seconds:** any app → 2 lenses → 4 users → pick one.
- **Drill B, three ideas before detail:** any problem → 3 one-liners in 90 seconds.
- **Drill C, flow in 2 minutes:** say all 6 steps out loud.
- **Redo the parking question** start to finish, timed, without help.

### Week 2: full timed mocks (3 a week)
- 2 **B2B** product sense questions (one Rippling-style, one Eightfold-style) + 1 consumer question to keep the habits general.
- Record yourself. Count filler words. Target: fewer than 5 "uh"s per answer.
- Goal: finish all 7 steps inside 30 minutes **without prompts**.

### Week 3: level and company-specific prep
- **Senior signal:** practise "What should X build?" strategy questions, and lead with strategy for 3–4 minutes.
- **Okta:** identity basics (SSO, MFA, passwordless, workforce vs customer identity), B2B personas (IT admin, developer, end user, security buyer), B2B metrics (activation, time to integrate, seats, net revenue retention).
- **Eightfold AI:** AI product sense (how to measure AI quality, bias and fairness in hiring, human-in-the-loop, hiring AI audit laws in the US and EU).
- **Rippling:** how one employee record connects HR, IT and finance; what breaks when an employee joins, moves or leaves; admin time saved as a metric.
- **Leadership stories:** 5 STAR+ stories at **team-level scope**. The scope of the story signals the level.
