# Introducing Yourself: Do's and Don'ts

> *"Tell me about yourself"* is a question you can expect in every interview for the rest of your life. It also comes as: *"Tell me a little bit about your background"* and *"Walk me through your resume."*

Call your answer to all of these your **personal pitch**. The interview can be won or lost in the first few moments — and the pitch is where that plays out.

---

## Why Companies Ask This

Interviewers are busy. Most candidates are duds. This question exists so the interviewer can quickly determine: *"Is this interview going to be worth my time?"*

**Research on first impressions:** Random observers with no domain expertise were asked to watch interviews and predict who would get offers. They were accurate. Researchers kept slicing the video shorter — down to just the first few seconds, the candidate introducing themselves — and predictions remained accurate. Even on mute.

First impressions are not just about the words you say. They are about your presence, body language, and delivery.

> **Your pitch is your one chance to make that first impression intentionally.**

---

## The Don'ts

All of these, when committed, can be microsignals for downleveling. Avoid them.

| Don't | Why It Hurts |
|---|---|
| **Make your pitch too lean** | Leaves the interviewer free to ask about anything — including the things you're weakest on |
| **Monologue** | A pitch that goes too long loses the interviewer before you've said anything interesting |
| **Start with "My name is..."** | Signals junior, scripted energy — they know your name |
| **Dump a laundry list of technologies** | A pitch is not a job description; listing every tool you've touched adds no signal unless every item maps directly to the company's stack |
| **Go deep on why you got into the field** | If you have relevant professional experience, this is almost never worth time; if you're breaking in, one sentence max |
| **Use sensational language** | Words like "coolest" read as overexcited and unpolished; use "cool" — it accomplishes the same thing more credibly |

### Example of a Bad Pitch

*(Candidate interviewing for a senior software engineering role at Google)*

> *"My name is Kevin, and I graduated from UC Berkeley with a degree in Computer Science.*
>
> *I got into computers because my dad bought me a C++ book in middle school. That was like 20 years ago, and my passion has not let up...*
>
> *I work at Microsoft now. My tech stack is .Net/C#, GraphQL, Visual Basic, Microsoft Azure, and Microsoft SQL Server. I'm working on Microsoft MSN, which you've probably used at least once in your life...*
>
> *Before that I worked at LinkedIn. At LinkedIn, I used technologies such as Vanilla JavaScript, jQuery, Ember, Java, Amazon S3, Memcached, and Hadoop. One of the coolest things I did there was introducing a quick help widget...*
>
> *I have plenty of other interesting projects to talk about, but do you want me to go further in the past or is this enough? By the way, this covers about eight years of my work experience."*

**What's wrong:** Starts with name. Goes deep on origin story. Dumps full tech stacks. Wanders. Ends by asking the interviewer to manage the conversation. No clear impact. No breadcrumbs. No hook.

## The Do's

| Do | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| **Open by stating your level** | *"I'm a senior PM..."* — sets the anchor immediately, signals confidence, and makes leveling easy for the interviewer |
| **Leave breadcrumbs** | Mention things you want to be asked about later; your pitch is a menu that should make the interviewer hungry for the meal |
| **Flex scope with lean, dense descriptions** | Name the type of work, scale, and complexity — not a list of tools |
| **Inject impact and metrics** | *"$1.6M in additional annual ad revenue"* is memorable; *"improved loading time"* is not |
| **Personalize it to the company** | Include something so specific to this company that it would make no sense in any other interview |
| **Proactively answer questions you'll be asked** | Signal your answer to *"What are you looking for?"* and *"Why this company?"* before they're even asked |
| **End with an invitation** | *"I can go into more detail on whatever you'd like to hear more about"* — hands control back gracefully and signals confidence |

### Example of a Strong Pitch

*(Same candidate, same role, same company)*

> *"I'm a senior software engineer, currently at Microsoft. If you use Edge as your browser, when you open a new tab you're seeing my work. One recent project was introducing a news feed that optimized the loading time and ended up bringing in $1.6 million of additional annual ad revenue. The greatest technical challenge on that project was performance optimization.*
>
> *Before Microsoft, I worked at LinkedIn. One cool thing I did there was introduce a quick help widget on LinkedIn's flagship — used by 1.5 million daily users. I built the core functionality where you can move it around the screen without any lag, which was technically challenging because I had to go beneath the framework and write that in Vanilla JavaScript to get those speeds.*
>
> *My experience also includes extensive work on graph traversal algorithms, some machine learning experiments, and mentoring teams of engineers.*
>
> *In my next role, I'm looking for established large-scale tech companies with meaty product engineering challenges and a team that needs a blend of empathy and leadership.*
>
> *I can go into more detail on whatever you'd like to hear more about."*

**What works:** Opens with level. Two specific projects with scale and impact metrics. One sentence on technical depth. Proactively signals what they're looking for (answers the follow-up before it's asked). Ends with an invitation. Tight and confident throughout.

## The Pitch Formula for PMs

Adapt the same structure for PM interviews:

```
[Level] + [Current company / product you own] + [Impact in one sentence]
↓
[Previous role] + [One specific project with scale or impact]
↓
[Optional: one sentence on depth — domain, skill, or leadership signal]
↓
[What you're looking for next — personalized to this company]
↓
"Happy to go deeper on whatever is most relevant."
```

**Example PM pitch:**

> *"I'm a Staff PM at [Company], currently leading the discovery and growth pillar for [Product] — which serves 40 million daily active users. Most recently I led a cross-functional initiative that redesigned our recommendation system; it drove a 22% lift in Day 7 retention and became the model for how the team now approaches onboarding across all surfaces.*
>
> *Before that I was at [Prior Company], where I launched a zero-to-one product in the enterprise workflow space. We went from 0 to $8M ARR in 18 months.*
>
> *I've done a lot of AI product work recently — specifically around LLM evaluation frameworks and how to define quality metrics when user satisfaction and model accuracy diverge. That's actually why [this company] is interesting to me — the way you're approaching [specific product challenge or strategy] is exactly the space I want to go deep in next.*
>
> *Happy to go deeper on whatever is most relevant."*

## Quick Reference

```
STRUCTURE
→ Open with your level (not your name)
→ 2 specific projects: current + previous, each with scale or impact
→ 1 sentence on depth or theme (domain, leadership, technical signal)
→ What you're looking for, personalized to this company
→ Invite follow-up

LENGTH
→ 90 seconds to 2 minutes max
→ Dense, not exhaustive — leave things to be asked about

BREADCRUMBS
→ Mention the most impressive or relevant things you want to be asked about later
→ If you include it in the pitch, you're inviting a question about it

PERSONALIZATION
→ At least one detail that would make zero sense in any other interview
→ Reference something specific about the company's product, strategy, or culture

AVOID
→ Starting with your name
→ Origin stories unless you're breaking into the field
→ Technology laundry lists
→ Any sentence that starts with "basically" or "so basically"
→ Sensational adjectives ("the coolest thing I've ever done")
→ Running over 2 minutes without an invitation for follow-up
```
