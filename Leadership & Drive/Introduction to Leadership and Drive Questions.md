# Introduction to Leadership & Drive Questions

> Leadership and drive (L&D) questions now account for half or more of the total evaluation at top companies. This lesson covers the five questions you're most likely to face and what interviewers are actually scoring when you answer them.

---

## What to Expect

L&D questions cluster around five recurring themes. **Don't prepare a unique answer to each one.** Get to know your work deeply enough that a smaller set of stories can flex to fit whatever's asked.

---

## The Five Recurring Themes

### 1. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a stakeholder or manager

According to dozens of verified reports from FAANG and Frontier AI labs, this is the **single most common leadership & drive question** in PM interviews. It shows up from multiple angles:

- *"Tell me about a time when you had to deal with conflicting priorities with your stakeholders and how you secured alignment with them."* — Stripe, Meta, Amazon + 5 more
- *"Tell me about a time when you disagreed or conflicted with leadership."* — Amazon, OpenAI, Microsoft + 1 more
- *"Tell me about a time you were confident in a solution and later realized it was wrong."* — Anthropic

---

### 2. Tell me about your most proud, most complex, or most impactful project

Used at Meta, Google, Amazon, and virtually every top company as a **launching pad to probe decision-making across the entire product lifecycle**.

- *"What product that you led are you most proud of and why?"* — Intuit, Robinhood, Microsoft + 7 more
- *"Tell me about the most complex project you have led."* — LinkedIn, Amazon, Zoom + 10 more
- *"Tell me about your most impactful project."* — Uber, Reddit + 2 more

---

### 3. Tell me about a time you took ownership and drove a project end-to-end

This is the **"drive" half** of leadership and drive. Other forms of this question include:

- *"Tell me about a time when you took ownership of a project you weren't originally responsible for."* — Amazon, Anduril
- *"Did you ever hit a major roadblock on a project, and how did you unblock the team?"* — Google DeepMind
- *"How have you used AI to bring about innovation and change either internally or externally to customers?"* — Amazon

---

### 4. Tell me about a time you received negative feedback

Netflix, Meta, and Google all ask this. **Netflix is particularly focused on this topic** — it evaluates whether you can give and receive direct, candid criticism.

> **From a verified Netflix interviewer:** *"We score candidates 0 to 5, and there is no standardized question bank. I dig into whatever thread seems most revealing."*

---

### 5. Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline with incomplete information

OpenAI and other Frontier AI Labs favor constraint-heavy questions because they map directly to their work environments. Other versions:

- *"Tell me about a time when you needed to convince a disagreeing team."* — Amazon, Google, Peloton
- *"Tell me about a time when you worked with incomplete data."*
- *"What would make you delay a major launch even under executive pressure?"* — OpenAI

---

## What Interviewers Are Looking For

| Signal | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Can you operate at the right scope for the level you're targeting — feature, team direction, cross-org initiative, or company-wide bet? |
| **Specific tension** | Can you name specific stakeholders, describe real tension, and explain how you changed the mind of someone who disagreed? |
| **Problem identification** | Did you identify and pursue problems rather than just execute what was handed to you — including problems nobody assigned you? |
| **Story structure** | Does your story hold up as a story? Real stakes, a genuine decision with a real alternative, and a close that lands on change rather than just a metric. |
| **Authenticity / Passion** | Do you come across as someone who lived the experience — not someone reciting a prepared answer? This is becoming the biggest differentiator now that AI-assisted prep has raised the floor on polish everywhere else. |

---

## Meeting the Senior Bar

| Level | What the Answer Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Competent** | Names a real stakeholder disagreement or a project you drove. States what happened. Lands on the result. |
| **Senior+** | Makes the scope of the story legible — who was involved, what changed at the org or company level, and why it mattered beyond the immediate project. Centers a real decision with a genuine alternative you considered, not a list of activities. Closes on a transferable insight rather than a metric. Describes problems the candidate identified and pursued themselves, not problems handed to them. |

---

## How to Prepare: The Story Bank Approach

Don't memorize one answer per question. Instead, build a small set of **flexible, deeply-known stories** that can be shaped to answer multiple question types.

A good L&D story bank has:
- **3–5 core stories** from different phases of your career or different types of projects
- Each story covers: the context, the tension/problem, your specific actions, the decision you made (and what you didn't), the outcome, and what you learned
- Each story can flex to answer at least 2–3 of the five theme areas above

> **The test of a good story:** Can you answer follow-up questions about it for 10 minutes without hesitation? If yes, you know it deeply enough. If not, pick a different story.
