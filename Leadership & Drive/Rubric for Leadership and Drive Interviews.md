# Rubric for Leadership & Drive Interviews

> Interviewers score each dimension on a four-point scale: **Strong No Hire → No Hire → Hire → Strong Hire.**

One dimension is rapidly becoming the biggest differentiator at the margin: **Passion.** As AI-assisted prep raises the floor on polish and structure, the candidates who stand out are those that seem genuinely invested and interested in the product space.

---

## Dimension 1 — Scope of Impact

Scores whether your story operates at the right altitude for the level you're targeting. Every seniority jump corresponds to a wider circle of impact: a feature, a team's direction, a cross-org initiative, a company-wide bet, an industry-level shift.

The most common failure here is not a lack of senior-level impact — it is that **the story does not make the scope visible.** A one-line description of a company-wide initiative reads the same as a one-line description of a feature fix. The level signal lives in the details, and details get cut from resumes.

> **Note:** Interviewers are not consciously scoring this dimension. They are pattern-matching against other candidates they have seen at each level. Your job is to make the pattern match obvious — not by claiming the scope, but by showing it through the specificity of what you influenced and who you influenced.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Strong No Hire** | The story describes a feature or task within a well-defined scope. The candidate completed work that was handed to them. No visible influence beyond their immediate team. |
| **No Hire** | The story involves real work but operates one or two levels below the target. The candidate contributed to something bigger, but it's unclear what they personally shaped. |
| **Hire** | The story operates at the expected altitude for the role. The scope is stated and visible in the details: the right number of teams, the right level of leadership, the right kind of decision. |
| **Strong Hire** | The story operates at or above the expected altitude. The candidate makes scope legible through concrete detail: who was involved, what changed at the org or company level, why this mattered beyond the immediate project. |

> **Senior bar:** Stories at this level should involve problems the candidate **identified**, not problems they were **assigned**. If every story in your bank begins with *"My manager asked me to..."* you're signaling one level below where you need to be.

## Dimension 2 — Stakeholder Management

Scores whether you can demonstrate real influence — specifically, whether you can name the people you moved, describe what they were resistant to, and explain how you changed their minds.

Stakeholder management is distinct from scope. A candidate can own an org-wide initiative and still have managed zero meaningful tension. What this dimension tests is whether you can operate in the **human complexity** of the work: competing incentives, disagreement with people above you, cross-functional partners who don't share your priorities.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Strong No Hire** | No identifiable stakeholders. Work is described as if it happened in isolation. Or stakeholders are named but there's no tension — everyone agreed, everything went smoothly. |
| **No Hire** | Stakeholders are mentioned but remain abstract. *"I worked with engineering and design"* — no specific friction, resistance, or moment of real misalignment. |
| **Hire** | The candidate names specific stakeholders, identifies what they disagreed about, and describes how the disagreement was resolved. The resolution is plausible and shows real judgment. |
| **Strong Hire** | The candidate names the specific person (role, level, function), articulates what they were protecting or optimizing for, explains the approach they chose and why not a different one, and connects the resolution to the outcome. The interviewer walks away understanding exactly how this person builds trust under friction. |

## Dimension 3 — Ownership

Scores the **"drive"** half of leadership and drive. Tests whether you identify and pursue problems rather than execute against a defined scope. At senior levels: problems nobody assigned you. Below Senior: carrying something through to completion when it would have been easy to hand it off or wait.

Ownership is distinct from scope. What's being scored is the quality of the initiative: did you wait to be told, or did you move? And when it got hard, ambiguous, or politically complicated, did you stay in it?

> **The twist variant that catches people:** *"Tell me about a time you pushed back on something but later realized you were wrong."* This is not a conflict question — it's an **ownership question**. It tests whether you own your mistakes as directly as you own your wins, and whether your self-assessment is honest rather than performed.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Strong No Hire** | Every story begins with an assignment or a manager's direction. The candidate executed well but cannot describe a time they identified the problem themselves or stepped in uninvited. |
| **No Hire** | There is some initiative, but it's within an already-defined problem space. The candidate did more than asked, but did not fundamentally reframe or expand the scope of what they took on. |
| **Hire** | The candidate describes a clear moment of initiative: identifying a problem no one assigned, unblocking something stalled, or staying in a situation that would have been easy to hand off. The stakes of that initiative are clear. |
| **Strong Hire** | The candidate took on something explicitly not their job, or actively stuck under another team, and drove it to a real outcome. The story shows they understood the risk — to their time, relationships, credibility — and chose to move anyway. |

> **Senior bar (Staff and above):** Companies like Anthropic, OpenAI, and Stripe are specifically looking for stories about problems the candidate **surfaced**, not just solved. The question underneath the question is: *Will this person tell us what we need to hear, or only what we asked for?*

## Dimension 4 — Communication

Scores whether your story holds up **as a story**, not just as a summary of events. Tests whether your opening creates a reason to listen, whether the stakes are legible, whether there is a real decision at the center rather than a list of activities, and whether your close lands on change rather than just a metric.

This is not about polish or fluency. A perfectly articulate candidate who describes activities instead of decisions scores No Hire on this dimension. What's being evaluated is whether the story has a **shape**: tension that builds, a judgment call at the center, and a resolution that says something about how you think.

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Strong No Hire** | The story has no discernible structure. Tasks described in chronological order. No stakes setup, no decision, no landing. The interviewer cannot reconstruct what actually happened. |
| **No Hire** | The story is legible but flat. Beginning, middle, end — but the middle is a list of activities rather than a decision. The close is a metric with no reflection. |
| **Hire** | The story opens with a clear hook, establishes why it mattered, contains a visible decision with at least one alternative the candidate considered, and closes with what changed. Follow-up questions feel easy because the structure is solid underneath. |
| **Strong Hire** | The opening creates immediate forward momentum. The stakes are specific and make the rest of the story impressive by implication. The key decision includes genuine tension. The close lands on an insight that is transferable beyond the specific project. The interviewer learns something from the answer. |

> **Senior bar:** What separates a good answer from a great one is the closing. *"We grew DAU 22%"* closes the story. *"We grew DAU 22%, and that changed how I think about what a roadmap is actually for"* opens a conversation. Senior candidates end with a **transferable insight**.

## Dimension 5 — Passion

Scores whether your story feels like something you **lived** rather than something you **prepared**. Not scored on enthusiasm or affect — a calm, measured candidate can score Strong Hire here. What's being scored is **texture**: the spontaneous detail, the moment of genuine uncertainty, the reflection that couldn't have been scripted because it's too specific to this exact experience.

This is the dimension that AI-assisted prep has the hardest time replicating. As average answer quality rises, interviewers are increasingly relying on this signal to separate candidates at the margin. **A polished but generic story is the new floor. What stands out is the story that only you could have told.**

| Score | What It Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Strong No Hire** | The answer sounds rehearsed start to finish. Language is generic — every sentence could appear in any PM's behavioral story. No spontaneous details, no genuine uncertainty, no reflection that reveals actual judgment. The interviewer feels no specificity of the person. |
| **No Hire** | The story is real but narrated from a distance. The candidate describes what happened without conveying why it mattered to them. The answer is correct but forgettable. |
| **Hire** | The candidate conveys genuine investment in the work. At least one or two details feel specific and unscripted. The reflection at the end is honest — including, where relevant, what they'd do differently. |
| **Strong Hire** | The story could only belong to this candidate. The details are too specific to be fabricated. The candidate's relationship to the work — what they found hard, what they found interesting, what they would change — is visible throughout. The interviewer remembers the answer after the loop. |

## Master Summary

| Dimension | Strong No Hire | No Hire | Hire | Strong Hire |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope of Impact** | Feature/task; work was handed to them | Real work, but one level below target | Expected altitude; scope visible in the details | At or above altitude; org-level change made legible |
| **Stakeholder Management** | No identifiable stakeholders; no tension | Abstract mentions of XFN; no friction described | Named stakeholder, real disagreement, plausible resolution | Named person + their incentives + approach chosen + why + outcome |
| **Ownership** | Every story starts with a manager's assignment | Initiative within a pre-defined problem space | Clear moment of uninvited initiative with visible stakes | Took on something explicitly not their job; understood the risk; drove it home |
| **Communication** | No structure; tasks in chronological order | Legible but flat; activities instead of decisions | Hook + stakes + decision + what changed | Momentum from the first sentence; genuine tension; transferable insight at close |
| **Passion** | Rehearsed and generic throughout | Real but narrated from a distance; forgettable | Genuine investment; 1–2 unscripted details; honest reflection | Story could only belong to this candidate; remembered after the loop |

## What to Self-Audit Before Your Interview

For each story in your bank, run this check:

```
SCOPE
→ Does my story make the scope visible through specific people and specific change?
→ Did I identify this problem, or was it assigned to me?

STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT
→ Can I name the specific person, their role, and what they were resistant to?
→ Can I explain what approach I chose and why not a different one?

OWNERSHIP
→ Was there a moment where I moved without being asked?
→ Can I honestly describe a time I was wrong — without performing humility?

COMMUNICATION
→ Does my opening create a reason to listen within the first two sentences?
→ Is there a real decision at the center with at least one alternative I considered?
→ Does my close land on an insight, not just a metric?

PASSION
→ Are there at least 1–2 details in this story that feel unscripted?
→ If someone who knows me heard this story, would they recognize it as mine?
→ Does the interviewer learn something from my answer?
```
