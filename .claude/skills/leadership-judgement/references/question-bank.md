# Question bank

Common behavioural questions grouped by family. In a mock, start with the family the learner's
story bank covers worst, and lead with a high-scope question (conflict or most impactful project) —
that's what real rounds do. Ask the main question, then 2–3 of the probes.

If the working directory has a `leadership-and-drive/` folder, its files are the source material for
these questions and for what interviewers score.

## Core questions (30)

### Conflict with a stakeholder or peer — the single most common family
1. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with an engineering lead or another partner.
2. Tell me about a time you had conflicting priorities across stakeholders. How did you get alignment?
3. Tell me about a time a partner team blocked your launch.

### Conflict with leadership
4. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager or leadership.
5. Tell me about a time you pushed back on an executive request.

### Disagree and commit
6. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision but committed to it anyway.
7. Tell me about a time you lost an argument at work. What happened next?

### Failure and being wrong
8. Tell me about your biggest professional failure.
9. Tell me about a time you were confident in a solution and later realised it was wrong.
10. Tell me about a launch that didn't hit its goal.

### Influence without authority
11. Tell me about a time you convinced a team that disagreed with you.
12. Tell me about a time you had to get something done through people who didn't report to you.

### Ambiguous 0→1
13. Tell me about a time you built or launched something from zero to one.
14. Tell me about a time you worked with very incomplete information.

### Hard prioritisation / saying no
15. Tell me about a time you said no to a senior stakeholder.
16. Tell me about the hardest trade-off you've made on a roadmap.

### Data-driven decision
17. Tell me about a time data changed your mind.
18. Tell me about a decision you made when the data was unclear or conflicting.

### Delivering under pressure
19. Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline.
20. What would make you delay a major launch, even under executive pressure? Tell me about a time.

### Ownership and drive
21. Tell me about a time you took ownership of something that wasn't your job.
22. Tell me about a project that was stuck. How did you unblock it?

### Most impactful / complex project (often becomes a deep dive)
23. What product that you led are you most proud of, and why?
24. Tell me about the most complex project you've led.

### Feedback
25. Tell me about a time you received negative feedback. What did you change?
26. Tell me about a time you gave difficult feedback to someone.

### Mentoring and developing others (weighted heavily at L5+)
27. Tell me about a time you helped someone on your team grow.

### Intro and deep dive
28. Tell me about yourself. / Walk me through your resume.
29. Why do you want to work here? / Why this role?
30. Walk me through a product you built, end to end. (Run with `deep-dive.md`.)

## Follow-up probes (use 2–3 per question)

- What did *you* specifically do?
- What was the impact? How did you measure it?
- Why that approach? What else did you consider, and why not that?
- What was the hardest trade-off?
- Who disagreed, and what were they worried about?
- How did you know you were right?
- What would have happened if it had failed?
- What would you do differently?
- What did the other person think of how you handled it?
- What did this change about how you work?

## Company-style variants

Use when the learner names a company. These are well-known public patterns; the exact questions vary
by interviewer.

**Amazon — Leadership Principles.** Each question maps to a principle; expect strict STAR, lots of
"what did *you* do?" and "what was the data?", and 2–3 stories per principle in the loop.
- Customer Obsession: "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer."
- Ownership: "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your area."
- Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit: "Tell me about a time you strongly disagreed with your manager."
- Dive Deep: "Tell me about a problem you solved by digging into the data yourself."
- Bias for Action: "Tell me about a time you made a decision without all the information."
- Deliver Results: "Tell me about a time you hit a goal despite a major setback."
- Earn Trust: "Tell me about a time you had to rebuild trust with a team."
- Invent and Simplify: "Tell me about a time you found a simpler way to solve a hard problem."
- Are Right, A Lot: "Tell me about a time your judgement was wrong."
- Think Big: "Tell me about a bold bet you proposed."

**Meta.** Conflict, most impactful project, and growth from feedback. Probes on scope and on how you
moved people across teams.

**Google.** Ambiguity, collaboration ("Googleyness"), and leading without authority. Expect "how did
you get alignment?" and "what did you learn?".

**Netflix.** Candour and judgement in context: giving and receiving direct feedback, decisions you
made without approval. Interviewers follow whatever thread is most revealing, with no fixed list.

**Frontier AI labs.** Constraint-heavy and judgement-heavy: incomplete information, shipping under
pressure, when you would *not* ship, being confident and later wrong, problems you surfaced that
nobody asked about.

**Stripe.** Conflicting priorities across stakeholders, rigour in written thinking, and ownership of
outcomes beyond your own team.
