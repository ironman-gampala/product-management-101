# "Tell me about yourself" and "Why this company"

Both questions come in almost every round, usually first. The interviewer is deciding in the first
minute whether this conversation is worth their attention, and what level you are. These two answers
set that anchor on purpose.

## Contents
1. The personal pitch (tell me about yourself)
2. Do's and don'ts
3. Why this company
4. Related questions
5. Running Intro mode

## 1. The personal pitch

Also asked as "Walk me through your background" and "Walk me through your resume". Same answer,
same structure. **60–90 seconds** (about 150–220 spoken words); never over two minutes.

```
  1. LEVEL + NOW       "I'm a [level] PM at [type of company], where I own [product / area]
                        for [scale]."
  2. RECENT IMPACT     one project, one number: "Most recently I [did X], which [moved Y by Z]."
  3. BEFORE THAT       one earlier role, one project with scale or impact
  4. THE THREAD        one sentence on depth: domain, skill or leadership signal
  5. WHAT'S NEXT       what you're looking for — tailored so it only makes sense for this company
  6. INVITATION        "Happy to go deeper on whichever part is most useful."
```

**Breadcrumbs.** Everything you put in the pitch is a question you're inviting. Mention your two
strongest stories in one line each so the interviewer asks about them.

**Tailor from the profile.** Pull level, current role, domain strengths and positioning line from
`my-profile/profile.md`, and the two biggest flagship stories from `my-profile/my-stories.md`. Line 5 changes for each
target company in the profile; lines 1–4 mostly stay the same.

**Career changers:** one sentence on the move ("I spent four years as an engineer, then moved into
product because…"), then treat the PM work as the main story.

## 2. Do's and don'ts

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Open with your level and current scope | Sets the level anchor before any story |
| Two projects, each with a number | Makes impact memorable and checkable |
| Name the type of work and scale, not a tool list | Scope is the signal, not tools |
| Say what you're looking for, tied to this company | Answers "why us" before it's asked |
| End with an invitation | Hands control back calmly |

| Don't | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| Start with "My name is…" | They know; it sounds scripted and junior |
| Tell your origin story | Burns 30 seconds on nothing they score |
| List every job in order | A resume read aloud; no hook |
| List technologies | A pitch is not a job description |
| Run past two minutes | They stop listening before your best part |
| End with "…so yeah, that's me" or "should I go further back?" | Asks them to manage you |
| Use "basically" or hype words ("the coolest") | Reads as unpolished |

**Shape of a strong PM pitch** (fictional example — for shape only, not your story):

> "I'm a senior PM at a mid-size logistics software company, where I own the shipment-tracking
> product for about 4,000 business customers. Most recently I led the move from email alerts to
> live tracking links, which cut 'where is my order' support tickets by about a third and became the
> template for two other teams.
>
> Before that I was at a consumer fintech, where I launched our first savings product from zero to
> about 200,000 users in a year.
>
> The thread through both is turning messy operational data into something a customer can trust at
> a glance. That's why this role caught my eye — you're making delivery promises visible to
> shoppers, and that's exactly the problem I want to go deeper on. Happy to go into whichever part
> is most useful."

## 3. Why this company

Hear: "Why do you want to work here?" Answer: **"What do you know about us, and how can you help?"**
Answering the literal question ("here's what you can do for me") sounds junior.

```
  1. ONE SPECIFIC THING   a product decision, launch, strategy move, paper, earnings-call line,
                          or a feature you used — something you couldn't say about a competitor
  2. THEIR NEED           what problem that signals they're trying to solve
  3. MY MATCH             the specific experience that maps to that problem
  4. ONE NUMBER           a metric or scope signal from your own work
  5. CLOSE TOWARD THEM    "I can help you do X", not "I'd love to grow"
```

45–60 seconds.

| Level | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| Ugly | "I've always been passionate about your mission." (About you; fits any company.) |
| Bad | "Great culture, smart people, cutting-edge tech, and my background fits well." (Claims research without showing any.) |
| Good | Names one specific thing they did, says what problem it signals, connects one piece of your work with a number, closes on what you'd help them do. |

**Research without inventing.** Use what the learner tells you, the job description they paste,
and well-known public facts about the company. If you're not sure a fact is current, mark it
`[verify: …]` and tell them to check it before the interview — a wrong "specific thing" is worse
than a general one.

Good places to look: the company's recent launches and changelog, the job description's first
three bullets, recent earnings calls or shareholder letters, engineering or research blogs, the
founder's or CPO's recent talks, and using the product for 30 minutes.

## 4. Related questions

| Question | What they want | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| "Why are you leaving?" | No drama, a pull not a push | What you've done there in one line → what you want next → why this role has it |
| "What are you looking for next?" | Fit with this role | Scope + problem space + team type, tied to their role |
| "Why product management?" | Conviction (career changers) | One moment that made you choose it, then evidence you're already doing the job |
| "Why this level?" | Self-awareness | The scope you already own, matched to the level's scope |

Never criticise your current employer. Never share a down-levelled competing offer.

## 5. Running Intro mode

1. Read `my-profile/profile.md` (level, role, strengths, target companies) and `my-profile/my-stories.md` (two biggest
   flagship stories). If missing, ask, one short question at a time: current role and scope, biggest
   result with a number, previous role and its best result, target company and role.
2. Draft the pitch using only their facts. Mark anything missing as `[placeholder]`.
3. Ask them to say it out loud and time it (or type it as they'd say it). Count words: 150–220.
4. Coach note: what landed, the top 2–3 cuts or fixes, one tightened line.
5. Repeat until it's under 90 seconds with a number in each project and a company-specific line 5.
6. Do the same for "why this company", one company at a time.
7. Offer to save the final versions under the "Intro" section of `my-profile/my-stories.md`.

Drills: say the pitch with a 90-second timer; say it in 30 seconds (forces the headline); swap only
line 5 for each target company.
