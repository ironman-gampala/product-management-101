# Introduction to Product Strategy

> As a PM, you'll make many strategic decisions — how to price products, whether to enter new markets, or how to address competitive threats. Product strategy interview questions assess your ability to analyze markets, evaluate competitive dynamics, and develop a strategy that supports the company's long-term vision.

---

## What to Expect

Product strategy questions are broad and open-ended. They come in several distinct types:

| Type | Example Question | Asked At |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic Analysis** | *"Imagine you're the CPO of Zoom, facing heavy competition from Google Meet. What would you do?"* | Google |
| **Go-to-Market** | *"You have technology that translates speech/text to animal language. You're a startup. What do you do?"* | OpenAI |
| **Pricing** | *"Should Amazon offer 'Amazon Prime Lite,' which offers just a few perks of Amazon Prime at a fraction of the cost? What would you include, and what would be the cost?"* | — |
| **Growth** | *"How would you 2X Grammarly's paid subscribers?"* | — |
| **Monetization** | *"You are the PM of Google Flights, and we need to monetize our strategy. What would you design?"* | — |
| **Market Entry** | *"Meta wants to build an education product. Design it."* | Meta |
| **New Technology** | *"Design the safeguards for an AI that takes actions on the user's behalf."* | OpenAI |

---

## The Framework

A reliable structure for any product strategy question covers five stages:

```
1. Clarify     → Understand the question and scope
2. Goals       → Define what success looks like for the company
3. Principles  → Establish the guiding constraints and priorities
4. Decisions   → Build options and land on a clear recommendation
5. Recap       → Summarize your reasoning and key risks
```

Later lessons cover the most common variants — **go-to-market, pricing, and growth** — each with their own tailored approach within this structure.

---

## What Interviewers Are Looking For

Strong strategy answers demonstrate five capabilities:

**1. Business model fluency**
Reason about the company's actual revenue drivers and use that understanding to guide your decisions — not just product intuition.

**2. Market and competitive awareness**
Build an accurate, specific picture of the competitive landscape, including how AI is reshaping the dynamics of the space right now.

**3. Clear recommendation**
Build a set of viable options and land on a defensible recommendation — even with limited information. Avoid ending on *"it depends."*

**4. Structured logic**
Each part of your answer should build on the last. The recommendation should *follow from* your analysis, not sit next to it as a separate thought.

**5. A genuine point of view**
Bring perspective shaped by real engagement with the industry. Generic answers signal surface-level prep.

> Depending on the exact question and role, you'll also want to demonstrate specific skills — such as reasoning about pricing models or identifying the right growth levers for a given product.

---

## Meeting the Senior Bar

| Level | What a Complete Answer Looks Like |
|---|---|
| **Solid** | Summarizes the product, company, and industry. Lands on a viable, defensible path. The logic is sound. |
| **Senior+** | Digs into context to identify *what matters most to the company right now* — including how AI or market shifts are changing competitive dynamics. Factors that into a recommendation that actively drives toward company goals. Proactively names what they'd do differently with more information, acknowledges key risks, and shows how the long-term vision would evolve. |

> The gap between solid and senior+ is not the depth of the feature list. It's the depth of the strategic framing that precedes it.
