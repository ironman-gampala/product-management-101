3. Assumptions (taken)

  • Horizon 12–18 months; primary markets US + urban India multi-device households.
  • Success = fewer nightly enforcement conflicts and higher sustained policy adherence — not maximizing minutes blocked.
  • Taken drill numbers: target parents already tried ≥1 parental control tool (~35–45%); ~60% abandon or loosen within 90 days due to friction / co-parent mismatch / kid bypass.
  • Typical household in ICP: 2 adults, 1–2 kids 8–14, ≥3 kid-reachable devices (phone + tablet + shared TV/console) spanning ≥2 OS vendors.
  • Kids 10–14 routinely bypass via secondary accounts, guest modes, friends’ devices, or cellular off Wi-Fi — product must assume adversarial ingenuity without becoming spyware theater.
  • OS vendors already ship Screen Time / Family Link; the gap is cross-vendor orchestration and two-adult consistency, not “another bar chart.”
  • Hard constraints: privacy, kid dignity, no always-on camera/mic/keystroke monitoring; regulatory and PR landmines if we ship surveillance wedge.
  • Taken commercial frame: can start as consumer subscription or platform feature; I will not let monetization hijack the JTBD in v1.

4. Transcript

— Clarify / user —

Interviewer

Design a better way for parents to manage kids' screen time across devices.

You

  • This is Product Sense — redesigning parental control for a multi-device household — not an RCA and not an ads monetization brief.
  • Primary user: the parent who sets policy (often one adult). Secondary: co-parent who must enforce. Tertiary: kid 8–14 whose dignity and learning agency matter.
  • I take a 12–18 month horizon. Success is sustained adherence and fewer conflict nights, not maximizing minutes blocked.
  • Decision object: what product surface and MVP beats fragmented OS tools without becoming spyware.

Board: sense · multi-device parents · adherence > blocks

Interviewer

Who exactly is hurting, and what is the job to be done?

You

  • Parent JTBD: when bedtime or homework windows hit and devices scatter, help me apply one coherent limit so I am not the nightly security guard.
  • Pain stack: (1) Apple Screen Time ≠ Google Family Link ≠ console pins ≠ smart TV profiles; (2) co-parent undoes rules; (3) kids bypass; (4) guilt dashboards show failure without a next action.
  • Emotional job: reduce shame and partner conflict — parents churn when the product makes them feel like bad cops.
  • Kid side-job (constraint, not primary buyer): fair rules they can predict; surprise hard-cuts teach them to treat the system as an enemy.

Board: JTBD: one coherent household policy without nightly fights

— Why now —

Interviewer

Why is this broken now versus five years ago?

You

  • Device count per kid rose post-pandemic; streaming, YouTube, and games live outside any single OS family graph.
  • Native tools already exist and still churn — taken ~60% loosen/abandon by D90 — so “ship another Screen Time” is not why-now.
  • Why now: fragmentation + dual-adult households + adversarial kids on cellular — the household graph problem got worse while OS tools stayed siloed.
  • Second-order trap: products that optimize for “minutes saved” create adversarial kids and shame parents into uninstalling, which looks like engagement on day 1 and death by D90.

Board: why now: fragmentation + dual-adult + bypass · not missing charts

— Constraints & principles —

Interviewer

What principles will you use to kill bad ideas?

You

  • Principle 1 — Cross-vendor reality: if it only works beautifully on one OS, it fails the household. Kills Apple-only clones as the whole answer.
  • Principle 2 — Two-adult consistency: policies need shared ownership and an audit of overrides. Kills single-admin dark patterns.
  • Principle 3 — Dignity over surveillance: no always-on content spying or keystroke capture. Kills “see every message” as the wedge.
  • Principle 4 — Reversible overrides with visibility: parents need grace windows without silently teaching kids rules are fake.
  • Principle 5 — Measure conflict nights and adherence, not blocked-minutes vanity.

Board: principles close doors before options

— Options —

Interviewer

Walk me through the option set.

You

  • Option A — Deeper single-OS Family Link / Screen Time clone with nicer charts. Easy distribution story; fails cross-device and dual-parent reality.
  • Option B — Household policy graph: map devices → kids → time/category policies; shared parent app; override + audit; weekly insight that suggests rule changes. Cross-OS via OS APIs where available plus a lightweight household Wi-Fi agent.
  • Option C — Kid-first gamified self-regulation (earn minutes). Helpful layer, weak as sole product because parents still need a hard stop for bedtime.
  • Option D — Network/router-only blocks. Strong on Wi-Fi, blind to cellular and friends’ devices — keep as a tactic under B, not the strategy.
  • I shortlist B as the strategy; C as a module under B; reject A as the product; D as infrastructure.

Board: shortlist: B policy graph | C module | REJECT A | D tactic

Interviewer

Why not just partner with Apple and Google and deepen native tools?

You

  • That is a distribution tactic, not a product strategy for the JTBD. Partnerships can help enrollment, but they do not give you a coherent household graph across TV/console/Android/iOS.
  • If I wait for OS vendors to solve cross-vendor, I am waiting for competitors to prioritize my edge case.
  • I will use OS APIs and family account hooks under B — I will not make “be a better Screen Time” the headline.

Board: partners = distribution under B, not the strategy

— Filter, steelman, close —

Interviewer

Kill what you will not do. Then steelman the best rejected option.

You

  • I reject Option A — polished Family Link clone. The pain is fragmentation across vendors and adults, not a missing bar chart on iOS. Cloning Apple loses on Android TVs and consoles by definition.
  • Steelman A: OS-native trust and App Store / Play distribution are huge; parents already have the icons. Defeat: distribution without solving the household graph leaves the core JTBD unmet; parents already churn from native tools (~60% loosen by D90 in taken data).
  • I reject Option C as the sole bet — bedtime still needs enforceable stops; gamification without co-parent visibility becomes another negotiation battlefield.
  • That leaves B as the serious candidate: a bounded household policy layer with dignity constraints.

Board: REJECT A parity trap · REJECT C-as-sole · pick B

Interviewer

What would you ship, how do you measure, who owns it, and when do you reverse?

You

  • MVP: (1) household device map with kid assignments, (2) shared policies for bedtime / homework / weekend, (3) one-tap override with reason + co-parent notify, (4) weekly insight: conflict nights, top bypass vectors, suggested rule tweak.
  • Integrations order: Android + iOS phones/tablets + household Wi-Fi agent first; TV/console connectors in phase 2 — not day-one scope bloat.
  • North star: weekly conflict-night rate per household (nights with ≥2 enforcement arguments or override spikes). Guardrails: override rate, kid-reported fairness pulse, D30/D90 retention, support tickets about “spyware feel.”
  • Owners: PM + Mobile eng + Network agent eng + Trust/Privacy review. 72-hour plan: instrument conflict/override events, lock privacy review checklist, recruit 30 dual-OS households for dogfood.
  • Reversal: if override rate stays >25% of sessions or fairness scores tank for two cycles, pivot UI from hard locks to soft budgets + negotiated extensions and kill invasive connectors.
  • I will not ship message reading or always-on camera/mic monitoring — that violates dignity and creates regulatory/PR landmines.

Board: MVP: map · policies · override audit · insight · NS=conflict nights↓ · reverse if overrides explode

Interviewer

One-line version for the VP of Consumer?

You

  • Parents do not need another Screen Time clone — they need a household policy layer that works across devices and co-parents, measures fewer fights, and refuses spyware theater.

6. 60-second skeleton

I'd treat this as Product Sense for a household policy OS, not a nicer Family Link. The job is coherent limits across devices and co-parents with fewer fights. Cloning one OS fails the JTBD — parents already churn native tools. Why now is fragmentation and bypass, not missing charts. I'd ship a device map, shared policies, audited overrides, and weekly insight — measure conflict nights and adherence — and reverse if overrides explode or kids feel surveilled. I will not ship spyware theater.
